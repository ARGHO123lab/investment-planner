Why We Created This Handbook

Financial calculators can make complicated calculations easier, but simply entering numbers into a calculator is not the same as understanding what those numbers mean.

A SIP calculator, for example, can estimate the potential value of regular investments. An EMI calculator can show the approximate repayment associated with a loan. An inflation calculator can demonstrate how purchasing power may change over time.

The purpose of this handbook is to go one step further. It explains the concepts, formulas and assumptions behind commonly used financial calculations so that you can understand the results rather than simply accept a number generated by a tool.

The examples are designed primarily for Indian personal-finance situations and use familiar Indian rupee amounts wherever appropriate.

A Calculator Is a Planning Tool, Not a Prediction

Financial calculators are useful because they can quickly perform calculations that would otherwise take considerable manual effort. However, the output is only as meaningful as the assumptions entered into the calculator.

For example, an investment calculator may use an assumed rate of return. That assumption does not guarantee that an investment will actually deliver the same return in the future.

Similarly, an inflation calculator does not predict future inflation. An EMI calculator does not determine whether a particular loan is suitable for you. A retirement calculator cannot know exactly how your future expenses, income or circumstances will change.

The most useful approach is therefore to treat calculators as decision-support and educational tools. Use them to compare scenarios, understand trade-offs and build a more informed financial plan.
