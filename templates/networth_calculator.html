What Is Net Worth?

Net worth is a measure of your overall financial position. It represents the value of the assets you own after subtracting the liabilities you owe.

Net Worth = Total Assets - Total Liabilities

Assets may include savings, investments, property, vehicles, retirement accounts and other valuable possessions. Liabilities may include home loans, personal loans, vehicle loans, education loans and credit card balances.

Why Track Your Net Worth?

📊 Monitor Financial Progress

Review your financial position periodically to understand whether your assets are growing and liabilities are declining.

🎯 Set Financial Goals

A clear starting point can make it easier to define realistic financial goals and track progress toward them.

💰 Make Better Decisions

Seeing assets and liabilities together gives you a broader view of your financial position.

🏦 Understand Debt

Tracking outstanding loans and other debts can help you understand how liabilities affect your overall financial position.

📈 Build Wealth Systematically

Monitoring net worth over time can help reveal whether your saving, investing and debt management habits are producing progress.

🎓 Improve Financial Literacy

Understanding assets, liabilities and net worth is a useful foundation for learning personal finance.

Example Net Worth Calculation

Priya's Example

Suppose Priya has the following assets:

  • Home: ₹60,00,000
  • Savings: ₹10,00,000
  • Mutual Fund Investments: ₹15,00,000
  • NPS and Retirement Savings: ₹20,00,000
  • Vehicle: ₹8,00,000
  • Total Assets: ₹1,13,00,000

Her liabilities are:

  • Home Loan: ₹40,00,000
  • Personal Loan: ₹5,00,000
  • Credit Card Debt: ₹1,50,000
  • Total Liabilities: ₹46,50,000

Net Worth = ₹1,13,00,000 - ₹46,50,000 = ₹66,50,000

In this example, Priya's estimated net worth is ₹66.5 lakh.

Practical Ways to Improve Your Net Worth

💼 Increase Earning Capacity

Career development, additional skills and suitable additional income opportunities may increase your ability to save and invest.

💳 Reduce High-Cost Debt

Prioritising expensive debt can reduce liabilities and free up future cash flow.

💰 Build Savings

Consistent savings can strengthen your liquid assets and provide a financial buffer for unexpected expenses.

📈 Invest Consistently

Long-term investing can help build assets, although returns are never guaranteed and investments carry risk.

🏠 Review Asset Values

Use reasonable current estimates instead of relying indefinitely on the original purchase price of an asset.

📊 Review Periodically

Quarterly or annual reviews can help you see whether your overall financial position is improving.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is net worth?

Net worth is the value of your total assets minus your total liabilities. The basic formula is: Net Worth = Total Assets - Total Liabilities.

How is net worth calculated?

Add the current value of your assets, such as savings, investments, property and retirement accounts. Then subtract outstanding liabilities, such as loans, mortgages and credit card balances.

How often should I calculate my net worth?

You can review your net worth quarterly or annually. The most important factor is using consistent definitions and reasonable valuations so that the numbers can be compared over time.

Does a negative net worth mean I am financially unsuccessful?

Not necessarily. A negative net worth can occur when liabilities are greater than assets, especially early in a career or after taking a large loan. Tracking the trend over time can be more useful than looking at a single number.

Use Net Worth as a Financial Progress Indicator

Net worth should not be viewed as a competition or as a single measure of financial success. It is simply a useful way to organise information about what you own and what you owe.

Over time, consistent saving, sensible investing, appropriate risk management and responsible debt management can influence your financial position. Reviewing your numbers periodically can make these changes easier to see.
