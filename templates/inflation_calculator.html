What Is Inflation?

Inflation describes a sustained increase in the prices of goods and services over time. When prices rise, the same amount of money generally buys fewer goods and services than it did previously. This reduction in purchasing power is one of the most important factors to consider when planning long-term financial goals.

For example, if a household spends ₹50,000 per month today and prices rise by an assumed 6% every year, the household may need substantially more than ₹50,000 per month in the future to maintain a similar level of spending. The actual increase in any particular expense will depend on the goods or services involved.

An inflation calculator does not predict the future. Instead, it allows you to test a specific inflation assumption and see how that assumption changes the amount of money required in the future.

How the Inflation Calculation Works

The calculator uses the standard future-value approach for estimating the amount required after applying an assumed annual inflation rate.

Future Amount = Current Amount × (1 + Inflation Rate)Years

For example, if ₹5,00,000 is exposed to an assumed 6% annual inflation rate for 10 years, the calculation compounds the 6% rate for each year. The resulting figure represents an estimated future spending requirement, not a guaranteed price.

Because inflation compounds, small differences in the assumed rate can produce significant differences over long periods. This is why retirement and education planning should ideally consider multiple inflation scenarios rather than relying on one number.

Why Inflation Matters in Financial Planning

01

Retirement

Retirement planning often spans several decades. Today's monthly household expenses may not remain unchanged over that period, so future expenses should be considered using an appropriate inflation assumption.

02

Education

Education is a long-term financial goal for many families. Estimating only today's tuition cost can underestimate the amount that may be required when the expense actually occurs.

03

Healthcare

Medical expenses can change differently from the overall inflation rate. Healthcare planning therefore deserves particular attention when estimating future retirement expenses.

Inflation and Purchasing Power

Purchasing power refers to the quantity of goods and services that a particular amount of money can buy.

If prices increase while the amount of money remains unchanged, purchasing power decreases. For example, ₹1,000 can purchase a different basket of goods in the future if prices have increased.

This is why financial planning should distinguish between nominal money values and real purchasing power.

  • Nominal value: The rupee amount shown on your account or statement.
  • Real value: The purchasing power of that amount after considering changes in prices.
  • Inflation-adjusted goal: A future financial target that accounts for an assumed increase in prices.

Important Limitations of Inflation Calculations

Inflation calculations are useful for financial planning, but they are based on assumptions. The calculator does not know what the prices of individual goods and services will be in the future.

Different categories can experience different rates of price increases. Housing, education, healthcare, food, transportation and technology may not move together.

The calculation also assumes a constant annual rate. Actual inflation can fluctuate from year to year because of economic conditions, supply and demand, commodity prices, monetary policy, exchange rates and other factors.

For these reasons, the calculator should be treated as a planning and educational tool rather than a prediction engine.

How to Plan for Inflation

  • Include inflation in long-term goals: Estimate future expenses rather than using only today's prices.
  • Use multiple scenarios: Test lower, base and higher assumptions.
  • Review your investments: Consider whether your expected portfolio return is comfortably above your inflation assumption.
  • Increase investments over time: Increasing contributions as income grows can help keep long-term goals aligned with rising expenses.
  • Maintain diversification: Different asset classes behave differently across economic environments.
  • Review your plan periodically: Inflation assumptions, income, expenses and financial goals can change over time.

Frequently Asked Questions About Inflation

What is inflation?

Inflation is the general increase in prices of goods and services over time. As prices increase, a fixed amount of money generally has less purchasing power.

How does an inflation calculator work?

The calculator applies an assumed annual inflation rate to a current amount over a selected number of years. It estimates how much money may be required in the future to represent the same spending requirement.

What inflation rate should I use?

There is no single rate that will accurately predict all future prices. Consider the purpose of your calculation, examine historical information and test multiple scenarios rather than relying on one assumption.

Does inflation affect retirement planning?

Yes. Retirement can last for several decades, meaning inflation can materially affect future living expenses. Retirement planning should therefore account for changing costs rather than assuming today's expenses will remain fixed.

Does this calculator predict future inflation?

No. It does not forecast future inflation. It simply applies the rate entered by the user to illustrate a potential future financial requirement.

Why does a small inflation difference matter?

Inflation compounds over time. Over a long period, a difference of even one or two percentage points in the assumed rate can create a substantial difference in the estimated future requirement.
