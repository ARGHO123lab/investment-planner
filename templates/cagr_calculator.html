📊 What is CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate)?

CAGR is the average annual growth rate of an investment over a specified period, accounting for the effect of compounding. Unlike simple returns, CAGR provides a more accurate picture of how your investment has grown year over year.

Example: If you invested ₹1 lakh in a mutual fund and it grew to ₹2 lakh over 5 years, the CAGR tells you the steady annual growth rate that achieved this result, which is approximately 14.87% per year.

📖 Understanding CAGR Results

CAGR vs Absolute Return

Absolute Return: The total percentage gain on your investment (Final Value - Initial Value) / Initial Value

CAGR: The annualized growth rate that smooths out year-to-year volatility. This is more useful for comparing investments over different time periods.

Example: An absolute return of 100% over 5 years equals a CAGR of approximately 14.87% per year.

🏆 CAGR Benchmarks in India

Fixed Deposits: 4-8% CAGR (Safe, guaranteed returns)

Government Securities (G-Secs): 5-7% CAGR (Very safe, government backed)

Inflation Rate: 5-6% CAGR (Your CAGR should exceed inflation to build real wealth)

Sensex (BSE): Historically 12-14% CAGR (Equity market returns, higher risk)

Nifty 50 (NSE): Historically 13-15% CAGR (Equity market returns, high risk)

Balanced Mutual Funds: 10-12% CAGR (Mix of equity and debt, moderate risk)

Equity Mutual Funds: 12-15% CAGR (High growth potential, higher risk)

💡 CAGR for Different Investment Types

📱 Short-Term Savings (< 1 Year)

Savings accounts and money market funds typically show modest CAGRs of 3-4%. Not meant for measuring long-term wealth creation.

🏦 Medium-Term Investments (1-5 Years)

FDs and bonds with expected CAGRs of 5-8%. Good for specific goals and capital preservation with steady growth.

📈 Long-Term Equity (5+ Years)

Stocks and mutual funds with expected CAGRs of 10-15%+. Higher volatility but potential for wealth creation over time.

🎯 Hybrid Investments

Balanced portfolios combining equity and debt, typically showing CAGRs of 8-12%, balancing growth and stability.

🔍 Key Factors Affecting CAGR

  • Asset Class: Equities typically have higher CAGR than bonds or savings accounts.
  • Market Conditions: Bull markets boost CAGR; bear markets reduce it significantly.
  • Time Period: Longer investment horizons typically show steadier CAGRs due to compounding.
  • Risk Taken: Higher CAGR usually comes with higher volatility and risk.
  • Diversification: Well-diversified portfolios often show balanced CAGRs with lower risk.
  • Cost of Investments: Lower fees and charges mean higher effective CAGR.
  • Taxation: Tax-efficient investments produce higher after-tax CAGRs.

💡 Smart CAGR Investing Tips

  • Long-term Focus: Don't chase short-term volatility. CAGR rewards long-term, disciplined investing.
  • Beat Inflation: Ensure your CAGR exceeds inflation rate (5-6%) to maintain purchasing power.
  • Compare Fairly: Always compare CAGRs across the same time period to make valid comparisons.
  • Consider Risk: Higher CAGR doesn't always mean better investment if it comes with excessive risk.
  • Regular Review: Monitor your portfolio CAGR annually to ensure it aligns with your goals.
  • SIP vs Lumpsum: Use CAGR to compare returns from systematic investments vs one-time investments.
  • Rebalance Regularly: Periodic portfolio rebalancing helps maintain target CAGR and risk levels.
