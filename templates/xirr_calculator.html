What is XIRR and Why Does It Matter?

CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate) assumes you invested a lump sum and made no additional investments. But in reality, most investors use SIPs (Systematic Investment Plans), add bonus money, or withdraw funds at different times.

XIRR is designed specifically for this. It calculates your annualized return considering every investment and redemption on their exact dates. This gives you an accurate picture of your portfolio's real performance.

Example: If you invested ₹10,000 in January, ₹10,000 in February, and ₹15,000 in March, XIRR accounts for the timing of each investment. A simple CAGR calculation would be inaccurate.

XIRR vs CAGR: What's the Difference?

  • CAGR assumes a single lump sum investment with no additional cash flows. It's useful for benchmarks but doesn't reflect most real portfolios.
  • XIRR accounts for every investment and withdrawal on their actual dates. It's the most accurate measure for personal portfolios with multiple transactions.
  • For portfolios with monthly SIPs and occasional additions, XIRR is always more accurate than CAGR.
  • Use our CAGR Calculator to compare and understand the difference.

Common Investment Scenarios for XIRR

  • 🏦 Mutual Fund SIPs - Track returns across monthly investments
  • 📈 Direct Stock Investments - Multiple buy-ins at different prices
  • 🏠 Real Estate - Down payment, additional investments, rental income
  • 💰 Lump Sum + SIP Mix - Combined portfolio with different investment styles
  • 🔄 Portfolio Rebalancing - Regular additions and reductions across funds
